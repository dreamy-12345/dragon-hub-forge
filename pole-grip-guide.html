The one rule

Never clamp or screw onto bare carbon. Something solid must be inside the pole under every clamp and every screw.

Why it matters

  • A 22 mm carbon staff tube is usually 22 × 19 mm. The wall is only about 1.5 mm thick.
  • Carbon is strong along its length, but weak when you squeeze it from the side.
  • A clamp or grub screw on a hollow tube crushes the fibres. The tube can then snap mid-spin.
  • Carbon cracks before it squeaks. You won't hear a warning.

Why printed TPU hubs are different

  • TPU stretches. Its crush ribs squeeze the pole gently, so it grips without a clamp.
  • Metal and rigid plastic hubs don't stretch. They need a clamp, and a clamp needs support inside.

Which method do I need?

Metal hub at a pole end→ 1. End Expander (best)
Hub along the pole, or flat CNC plate→ 2. Clamp Collar + Pole Insert
Fire spoke hub→ 3. Split clamp + Pole Insert
Printed TPU hub→ 4. Crush ribs (the forge default)
Rather buy parts?→ 5. Buy-in options

The methods are listed best first. If two fit, pick the higher one.

1. End Expander recommended

What it is: a plug that goes into the pole end and spreads out against the inside.

Why: it pushes outwards from inside, so nothing squeezes the tube. That makes it safe for carbon.

Best for metal hubs that sit at the ends of the pole.

Print (forge: Pole fittings → End Expander)
  • The slotted sleeve with its flange cap. The slots open at the bottom, and the bore narrows towards the top.
  • The cone wedge, with its M6 nut trap.
  • Material: PETG or nylon (PA), 100% infill, 4 or more walls.
Buy
  • 1 × M6 bolt. The forge shows the length, about 45 mm.
  • 1 × M6 nut and 1 × M6 washer.
  • 4 × M4 bolts and 4 × M4 all-metal lock nuts, to hold the hub plate on.
Fire hubsOn a fire hub the expander sits right at the hub, close to the heat. Print it in nylon, or have it turned in aluminium. A machinist can work from the same STL.
Steps: fit the End Expander
  1. Push the M6 nut into the wedge's nut trap. It should sit flush.
  2. Put the wedge under the sleeve, narrow end up.
  3. Put the washer on the M6 bolt.
  4. Pass the bolt down through the cap and the sleeve.
  5. Screw it into the wedge's nut by two or three turns only. Keep it loose for now.
  6. Slide the sleeve into the pole end.
  7. Push it in until the flange sits flat on the pole end.
  8. Tighten the M6 bolt. The wedge pulls up and spreads the fingers against the inside of the pole.
  9. Stop at snug.
  10. Lay the hub plate on the flange and line up the 4 holes.
  11. Fit the 4 M4 bolts and lock nuts.
  12. Tighten them a little at a time in a cross pattern, so the plate pulls down evenly.
Check before use
  • Twist the hub by hand. It must not turn.
  • Pull the hub along the pole. It must not move.
  • Look at the gap between flange and pole end. There should be none.
  • All 4 M4 nuts are tight.
Good to know (bolt pattern)
  • The flange has 4 holes on a 44 mm bolt circle.
  • That is the same pattern as the forge's CNC hub plates' Collar bolts, so any of those plates bolts straight on.
  • To take it off, loosen the M6 bolt a few turns, then tap its head gently to drop the wedge.

2. Clamp Collar + Pole Insert

What it is: a split collar that pinches the pole, with a solid insert inside the pole to take the squeeze.

Why: the hub can sit anywhere along the pole. The insert stops the clamp crushing the tube.

Best for hubs part-way along the pole, and for flat CNC plates.

Print (forge: Clamp Collar and Pole Insert)
  • The Clamp Collar: a split collar with a flange. The flange has 4 × M4 holes on the 44 mm circle. A pinch bolt crosses the split, with an M5 nut trap on one side.
  • The Pole Insert: a plug that slides inside the pole, under the collar.
  • Material: PETG or nylon (PA), 100% infill, 4 or more walls. Use nylon near fire.
Buy
  • 1 × M5 × 30 bolt, 1 × M5 nut and 2 × M5 washers.
  • 4 × M4 bolts and 4 × M4 lock nuts (all-metal near fire).
  • A long piece of string and a thin dowel, to move the insert.
Steps: fit the collar and insert
  1. Decide where the hub will sit. Mark that spot on the outside of the pole.
  2. Measure from the pole end to the mark.
  3. Tie the string to the insert.
  4. Push the insert into the pole end.
  5. Move it along inside the pole to the mark. Push with the dowel, pull back with the string.
  6. Mark the string at the pole end, so you can always find the insert again.
  7. Slide the collar on and line it up over the insert.
  8. Bolt the hub plate to the collar flange with the 4 M4 bolts and lock nuts.
  9. Tighten those 4 in a cross pattern, a little at a time.
  10. Put the M5 nut in its trap.
  11. Fit the M5 bolt with a washer under the head.
  12. Tighten the pinch bolt until the split closes a little. Stop at snug.
Check before use
  • The insert is right under the collar. Check with the string mark.
  • The split is still open a little. If it's fully shut, the collar is too big or the insert is too small.
  • The hub can't twist or slide by hand.
  • No white marks or creaks from the carbon.

3. Split-clamp spoke hub

What it is: the fire spoke hub clamps itself onto the pole with one pinch bolt.

Why: no extra collar needed. The hub is its own clamp.

Forge setting
  • Spoke hub → Pole grip → split clamp (pinch bolt).
  • This cuts a slit from the bore to the edge, between two spokes.
  • One M5 pinch bolt crosses the slit. Machined hubs are tapped for it. Printed practice hubs have a nut trap.
  • Also print a Pole Insert. Always use one with this method.
Buy
  • 1 × M5 bolt and a washer. Add an M5 nut for a printed practice hub.
Steps: split-clamp hub
  1. Fit the Pole Insert where the hub will sit (steps 1 to 6 of method 2).
  2. Slide the hub on over the insert.
  3. Turn it so the spokes point where you want them.
  4. Fit the M5 bolt across the slit, with a washer.
  5. Tighten until the slit closes a little. Stop at snug.
Grub screw optionThe other Pole grip setting, grub screws, uses two M5 grub screws pressing on the pole. A grub screw point is the harshest thing you can put on carbon. Use it only with a Pole Insert inside.

4. Crush ribs (printed TPU hubs)

What it is: small ribs inside the hub's bore squeeze onto the pole.

Why: TPU stretches, so the ribs grip gently. No clamp is needed, and it's safe for carbon. This is the forge's default.

Print
  • The fit-test ring first. It's quick and shows if the ribs are right.
  • Then the hub.
Steps: crush-rib hub
  1. Print the fit-test ring.
  2. Push it onto the pole. It should go on firmly but by hand.
  3. Too loose? Make the fit tighter in the forge and print another ring.
  4. Too tight to push on? Make it looser and print another ring.
  5. When the ring is right, print the hub with the same settings.
  6. Push the hub onto the pole with a twisting motion.
Set-screwIf you add the optional set-screw, put a Pole Insert inside the pole under it first.

5. Buy-in options

Ready-made parts that do the same jobs. Search for these generic names.

"rubber expansion plug 19 mm" instead of the End Expander

What it is: a rubber bung that swells outwards when you tighten its bolt.

Why: it grips from inside like the End Expander, so it's carbon-safe.

  • Size it to the pole's inside diameter (19 mm on a 22 × 19 tube).
  • Rubber softens with heat. Keep it away from fire ends.
"flange shaft collar 22 mm" or "rigid flange coupling 22 mm" instead of the Clamp Collar

What it is: a metal split collar with a flange for bolting a plate on.

Why: stronger than a printed collar and fine near heat.

  • Always use it with a Pole Insert. Metal collars squeeze hard.
  • Check that its bolt holes match your plate before you buy.

How tight is tight?

Snug
  1. Tighten until the hub can't twist by hand.
  2. Then a quarter turn more.
  3. Stop. Carbon cracks before it squeaks.

Use a short spanner or hex key. Long tools make it too easy to overdo it.

Troubleshooting

Hub slips round the pole
  • End Expander: tighten the M6 bolt a quarter turn.
  • Clamp: the insert is missing or too small. Reprint it with a lower Slide fit.
  • Clamp collar: add a strip of grip tape under the collar.
Hub wobbles
  • Collar not square: loosen the plate bolts, then tighten them evenly in a cross pattern.
  • Pole end not cut square: sand it flat, then refit.
Creaking or white marks on the carbon
StopThe fibres are crushed. Replace that pole section. Next time, always use an insert.
The expander won't go in
  • Sand the sleeve lightly, then try again.
  • Or reprint it with more clearance.
Bolts loosen with spinning
  • Near fire: use all-metal lock nuts.
  • Away from heat: nyloc nuts are fine.
  • Non-fire parts: add a drop of medium thread-locker.
  • Re-check every bolt every session.

Before every session

  1. Twist every hub by hand. None should turn.
  2. Pull every hub along the pole. None should slide.
  3. Check every M4 plate bolt is tight.
  4. Check every M5 or M6 clamp bolt is tight.
  5. Check the string marks: every insert is still under its clamp.
  6. Look at the carbon next to each hub. No white marks, no cracks.
  7. Listen as you flex the staff. No creaks.
  8. Near fire: all-metal lock nuts only, and no printed part is soft or warped.
  9. Anything wrong? Fix it before you spin.

These are starting points, not specifications. If you're unsure, ask an experienced spinner to check the prop before you use it.
